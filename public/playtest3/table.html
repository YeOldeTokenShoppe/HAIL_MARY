Playtest 3 rules · One Money

Be the first to hold $1 billion in cash. There is one money. You spend it to play cards and you count it to win. Unsold Assets count for nothing until you sell them.

Choose Devotion or Degen, take a seat, then select Deal opening hand. Before the deal, choose your starting Trader from your deck’s juniors, the Traders who cost $20M or less. You open the game as that Trader, free. To become a costlier Trader, play them from your hand: switching costs the new Trader’s price and your main action, and the Trader you were is discarded. Each player then starts with five cards from the remaining 39 and $50M.

Your turn

  1. Start of turn. Things that stand on your desk act first: The Cow milks, The Rooster rolls, a planted Seed Round blooms, The Nativity Scene gains a figure, and you may bury your Trader in The Catacombs. The table handles the automatic ones.
  2. Collect income. Your seated Trader earns their Liquidity, plus their Credibility while in good standing. An empty or disabled seat earns $10M. Under a Liquidity Trap, everything you collect piles on the Trap instead until it is cleared.
  3. Draw a card. Tap your deck. An Event marked “fires on draw” resolves immediately for everyone, then you draw a replacement. An Enigma Machine on your desk can intercept one.
  4. Choose one main action. Play a card and pay its cost, sell a holding, offer a card as a gift, pay $30M to clear one of your Calamities, attempt a Hail Mary, or pass. Some holdings give you another choice, such as uncorking The Cellar for a candle. Reading a card is free.
  5. Finish your turn. If you have more than seven cards in hand, choose cards to discard until you have seven.

Winning. The round is always played out. When it ends, anyone holding $1 billion or more wins; if both players are there, the richer one takes it.

Traders and the trinity

Every Trader carries three numbers. Liquidity (the drop) is what they cost to play and what they earn you each turn; each point is $10M. Credibility (under the halo) is the bonus they earn each turn while in good standing. Equanimity (the die) is their composure: when a rival plays a Calamity on you, roll the d8; on your seated Trader’s Equanimity or lower, the Calamity fails. It is a stat, not an ability, so a Quantum Hack does not switch it off.

Credibility has two states. A Trader is in good standing or in scandal. Any Calamity that lands on you is a strike: the first puts your Trader in scandal and stops their Credibility bonus; a second, while still in scandal, discredits them and they leave the desk, unless a card with Saving Face is spent from your hand or the Shroud is on the table. Lighting a candle returns a Trader to good standing, but not while a Scandal sits on your desk.

The earned halo. Once per Trader you can earn a halo, worth $10M more a turn in good standing. Offer a card from your hand to your rival as a gift, as your main action: if they accept, the halo is yours. They may refuse. Clearing a Calamity from your rival’s desk earns it too.

Assets and your portfolio

An Asset you buy goes into your portfolio. Starting on your next turn, you can use your main action to sell it for cash. Inspect the holding to see what it sells for today: some grow each round, The Avocado ripens and then rots, The Holy Grail is worth more while three candles burn, and a gamble lets you roll the d8 for its full price or take a smaller safe price. Rug Pull blocks sales until cleared. A Bear Market caps what one card can pay at $75M, and liquidates a leveraged Perp. The Lowrider keeps your portfolio out of the Whale’s and the Wheel’s reach.

Events, Calamities, Remedies and Relics

Some Events fire when drawn. Others stay in your hand until you play them: most pay out at once, a Seed Round is planted and blooms four rounds later, The Ponzi pays from your rivals’ holdings. A Calamity played on a rival sits on their desk until cleared; a Sacred Heart can cancel one as it lands, Equanimity can make it fail, and the Burro kicks one back where it came from. Use a matching Remedy, a Miracle Tortilla, or pay $30M to clear one from your own desk.

Relics give ongoing effects. You hold two at a time, three with the Motherboard, which takes no slot itself. Graces are offerings at the shrine: each lights a candle.

Locations

A Location is a place you build: one stands per player, it takes no Relic slot, and it is never sold. Building a second tears the first down. The Nativity Scene gains a figure each turn and pays on the third; The Catacombs turn a Trader into cash; The Chart Cathedral pays $25M whenever anyone lights a candle.

Candles and the Hail Mary

Candles are lit by Graces, the Feast, the Nativity and The Cellar, up to three. Once per game, you may spend your main action to attempt a Hail Mary. With zero, one, two, or three lit candles, the odds are 1, 2, 3, or 4 winning faces on the d8; The Fountain adds one winning face per use, up to 5 in 8, and a Virtue Signal on you counts your candles as none. Your desk shows the odds after card effects. An answered prayer clears your Calamities and pays $200M. A miss spends the attempt, unless the Dashboard Figure rolls again.

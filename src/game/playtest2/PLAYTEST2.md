# RL80 R-cana — Playtest 2: The Trading Desk

A delta on the First Playtest (see `../playtest1/PLAYTEST1.md` for everything not mentioned here). Playtest 2 asks one question: **does the game get better when your Trader is your paycheck?**

## What changed

- **Due Diligence is an open book** (2026-11-16): *the top card of your deck is face up to you; once on your turn, you may put it on the bottom.* La Investigadora no longer asks a question on every draw, which in play was annoying and at first confusing. At the table the top card lies face up on your deck while she is seated, with **View** and **Bury** under it; Bury works once a turn, before or after your draw, and nobody is prompted. An on-draw Event can be seen coming and buried. A Quantum Hack on the seat turns the card face down again. Bots decide before their draw, by the same rule as before, so the sim is unchanged (1,500 games: Devotion 33%, Degen 34%, 15.0 rounds). Her printed face already reads *once a turn, peek at the deck's top card; you may send it to the bottom*, so no re-render is needed. Engine: `topCard`, `canBury`, `bury` in pt2-sim.js.
- **El Gato's Clean Sweep rolls every turn** (2026-11-16): *when seated, and at the start of each turn on a roll of 4+, knock one Calamity off your table, free.* The arrival sweep is kept; the roll is new, and the cat picks which Calamity goes. It gives him a job when he opens the game at a clean desk, where the old text did nothing. A Quantum Hack on the seat stops him. 1,500 games: as an opener he goes from 32% to 34% of his seats; Devotion 33%, Degen 34%, 15.0 rounds. A once-per-seating version (the first Calamity to land is swept) and rolls of 3+ and 5+ all landed within a point of this. **The face needs re-rendering** for the new text: `npm run rcana:faces -- el-gato`, then `npm run pt2:build`.
- **You play as a Trader; you do not hire one** (2026-11-16): the starting-Trader text at the table now says *open as* and *become*, not *hire*. Players aspire to be the Trader. Switching still costs the new Trader's price and a main action, as a Pokémon retreat does. (The sim's `hires` counter keeps its name.)
- **What each ability is worth** (2026-11-16, bots; each Trader's copies swapped for a same-statline Trader with no ability, 1,500 games): El Unicornio's Compound Interest carries Degen — without it Degen falls from 35% to 16% of its seats. La Influencer's Star Power is worth about 2 points to Devotion. The other eight are within a point of nothing. Degen wins 52% when the Unicorn is seated by round 3 and 12% when he never is (a third of games each). The stars are not level: the game is close to decided by one draw. Not yet addressed.
- **The starting Trader** (2026-11-16): *before the deal, choose one Trader from your deck and seat them, free. Only a junior may open at the desk — cost 2 or less; the stars, who cost 3, are hired from your hand at their price.* The game is then played from 39 cards, and nobody begins with an empty seat. Devotion opens with El Monje, El Gato or El General; Degen with El Diablito, El Gambusino or La Abuela. Bots open with one of their juniors at random. This replaces the morning's opening rule (below): with the seat filled, the redeal has nothing to do.
  - **Should the starter cost anything? No — Liquidity is the wrong lever.** Charging the starter's cost out of the opening 5 moved no matchup by more than about 3 points, and neither did double or triple the cost (opening Liquidity anywhere from 0 to 6). What a Trader really costs is the main action and the wait to draw them, and a starter skips both.
  - **Why juniors only.** With any Trader allowed, the choice was no choice: El Unicornio (Degen) and La Influencer (Devotion) beat every other opener — Degen won 5–27% of its seats opening with anyone but the Unicorn and 56–65% with him against anyone but the Influencer — because a Prosperity engine seated from turn one runs all game. A head start in Prosperity for cheaper openers (50 or 100 per cost point) did not level it. Keeping the three-cost stars in the deck does: all six juniors win 31–37% of their seats, and hiring a star is still a decision with a price.
  - **Bot:** a better-paid Trader in hand is now hired when affordable (score 75, was 30). Without it bots sat on their junior all game and Degen fell to about 20%.
  - `STARTER_MAX_COST` in pt2-sim.js is the switch (2; `null` lets any Trader open). `--starters monje,diablito,gato` sets the opener per seat in the sim, which now prints a win rate by opening Trader. At the table the choice is made on the opening screen and kept in Table settings for the next deal.
  - 1,200 games: 3 players Devotion 32%, Degen 35%, 15.1 rounds; 2 players 49/51, 15.9 rounds; 4 players 26/24, 14.1 rounds. Hires 3.2 a game (3.6 before). No card values were changed.
  - Open: the seat can still empty after a discredit or a burial. (El Gato's Clean Sweep did nothing when he opened; fixed the same day, above.)
- **The opening rule** (2026-11-16, morning; superseded the same day by the starting Trader, above): *every opening hand holds a Trader — a five dealt without one goes back into the deck and is dealt again.* Pokémon's opening, for the same reason: a third of opening hands had no Trader (Devotion 37%, Degen 26%), the seat stood empty on 15% of all turns, nearly all of it before the first hire, and about 4% of Devotion seats never filled all game. The seat still starts empty and seating still costs the Trader's price and your main action; a vacancy after a discredit or a burial is untouched. Starting already seated was tried and withdrawn: hires fell from 3.5 a game to under 1 and the starting Trader decided the matchup (best-LIQ starter: Degen 53%) — held for a later Leader test. The redeal is logged at the table. 800 games: Devotion 33%, Degen 34%, 15.7 rounds, hires 3.6; a Trader-less opening hand is now 0%.

### 1. Traders carry three things: LIQ, GRACE, and an ability

Pokémon-style statlines, R-cana values:

- **LIQ** is your income. At the start of your turn you collect your **seated Trader's LIQ** instead of a flat 2. No Trader seated (or your seat is hacked)? You collect **1** — you're between Traders.
- **GRACE** is their reputation, their state of grace, and their health. Scandals drain it. At **zero, the Trader is disgraced**: discarded, seat empty, income back to 1 until someone else sits down.
- **One named ability** for every Trader, printed beside **ABIL80**.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|

| El Gato | 2 | 2 | 16 | When seated, and at the start of each turn on a roll of 4+, knock one hazard off your table, free. |

### 2. The active seat

El Unicornio's **Compound Interest** resolves at the start of its controller's
turn, before income and the draw. Only the active, enabled Trader earns it;
playing or swapping it into the active slot does not pay immediately. Quantum
Hack blocks it unless protected by the Halo. The payout uses normal banking
rules, including Bear Market’s cap and the victory check. Its cost, income and CRED
remain 3 / 3 / 20; the added ability still needs balance playtesting.
A paired 200-game check (seeds 1–200, two Greedy bots, alternating seats,
Devotion vs Degen) moved Degen from 107 wins without the ability to 127 with
it, and shortened the average game from 16.73 to 15.67 rounds. This is a bot
balance signal, not a claim of balance under human play.

El Diablito's **Devil’s Wager** resolves before normal income. Only the seated,
enabled Trader flips. Tails damages his own CRED using the normal disgrace and
Shroud rules; it can also end his spotless-reputation income bonus. An El Diablito in hand or under a Hack does not flip (the Halo still protects from the Hack).
The coin replaces his former Calamity rebate.

- One active Trader earns income and uses their ability. Spare Traders stay in hand.
- As your main action, pay the incoming Trader’s cost, discard the current Trader, and seat the new one.
- Quantum Hack disables the *seat* (ability and LIQ) until cleared, whoever sits in it. The Halo still protects.

### 3. The scandal economy — Grace goes down, and comes back up

Drains:
- **El Escándalo** (new hazard, cost 2, Degen ×2): the seated Trader loses **5 Grace** when it breaks, and **2 more** at the start of each turn until cleared.
- Disgrace and the Hack work as above.

Restores:
- **Lighting a candle restores 3 Grace** to your seated Trader. Public piety launders reputation.
- **The Press Release / El Comunicado** (remedy, cost 1, Devotion ×2; was El Publicista until 2026-11-10 — the Remedies row is objects, not people): clear a Scandal — or play it as polish: your seated Trader regains **5 Grace**.

Upside:
- **A spotless reputation pays**: while your seated Trader is at **full Grace** (and not hacked), they earn **+1 Liquidity** on top of their LIQ. Grace damage persists after a Scandal is cleared — the scar outlives the story — so polishing back to full has a real payoff.

## The set and the number 80

**Neither was an Asset** (2026-11-12). **The Motherboard** is a Relic (cost 2 — *expansion slots: the board takes no Relic slot, and you may hold a third Relic*; La Placa Madre; Degen ×1). (First draft had it take a slot to grant one — a net zero the bots never bought.) **The Neural Cathedral** opens a new row, **Locations** (engine type `location`, printed under the Artifact class with its own subtype like Remedy and Grace): places you build, one standing per player, a new one replaces the old, no Relic slot, never sold. The Cathedral itself was then replaced by **Las Lágrimas** (Grace, cost 1 — *to the shrine: light a candle*; Saving Grace 4, the strongest rescue in the hand; Devotion ×1 for one Ofrenda), so the Location row stands empty in Genesis: the engine and the table support it, the gallery hides an empty row, and the Stadium-style shared version with more places is second-set material. Collectibles went back into Devotion for the Asset slot. Rows: Traders 12, Assets 11, Events 20, Hazards 8, Remedies 7, Relics 13, Graces 9.

**La Celebridad is The Influencer / La Influencer** (2026-11-12) — the card's own ability text always called her the influencer; the name caught up. Key `celebridad` and the `the-celebrity` art folder are unchanged.

**Numbering (2026-11-12).** All 80 are numbered 1–80 — no numbered/special split, since rarity is deferred (every new face is Common until the set earns tiers). `src/game/rcana/set/numbers.js` reserves one number per design: the 40 faces already rendered keep their provisional numbers (7–49), and the 40 text cards were dealt the open slots (1–6, 8–10, 50–80) in row order. Lint checks every face against the table, so a number cannot be handed out twice as art arrives.

The lotería set stays **54 numbered designs**; specials (Moral Hazard, The World, Flash Crash…) ride unnumbered. Target for the RL80 Genesis set: **80 cards total** — 54 numbered + 26 specials. Decks remain 40, constructed from the set.

## What this playtest needs to answer

- Does income-through-Traders make inhabiting an active Trader feel essential without making the time between Traders miserable?
- When is replacing the active Trader worth the cost and main action?
- Is 3-Grace-per-candle the right lifeline, or does devotion trivialize scandals?
- Does La Estrella (GRACE 8) die too fast? Does anyone ever disgrace at all?
- Sim baseline at ship: 2p devotion/degen 48/52, ~21 rounds, hires 3.3/game, disgraces 0.14/game, cash sales up 6× over Playtest 1.
